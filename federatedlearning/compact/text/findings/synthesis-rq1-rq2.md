# Text campaign synthesis — RQ1 and RQ2

**Coverage:** 13 selected, executed AG_NEWS/MLP notebooks; source manifest records the selected notebooks, plots, box reports, and current stratification-guide fingerprint. The cases are overlapping views/strata, not a disjoint partition of all campaign runs.

## RQ1 — Tradeoffs and design allocation

Across the views, higher validation accuracy and lower average total time define `L-S` as preferred. Eleven cases have observed `L-S` support; the two HDH-only views do not, so that preferred region remains unobserved there. `L-M` is also prominent, while the other occupied cells are sparse and vary by slice. The `S/M/L` symbols are numeric bins, not quality ratings. Each case reports complete train and held-out cell counts including zeros.

The comparison tree in the stratification guide supports four distinct claims:

- **Client-count all-configuration views (4, 8, 10 clients):** compare complete ON/OFF combinations within fixed client-count slices. They do not pool client counts, but remain all-environment observational views. The `L-S` held-out precision is 1.0 in the supported PRIM boxes, while coverage varies (about 0.19 at four clients, 0.65 at eight, and 0.63 at ten); the cases’ within-configuration allocation tables should be read separately because these views overlap the wider campaign.
- **Configuration profiles (baseline, CS-only, MC-only, HDH-only):** each fixes one pattern configuration and pools clients 4/8/10. They characterize profiles; they do not estimate pattern effects against a matched control. Baseline and MC-only have perfect `L-S` PRIM validation on 9/9 held-out targets; CS-only is weaker (7/20 targets captured at 0.78 precision). HDH-only has no `L-S` observations, so its strict preference cannot be modeled; its occupied outcomes are `L-M` and `L-L`.
- **Stress-only views:** isolate one pattern at a time and vary its stress levels while pooling client counts. CS-only has observed `L-S`, but its held-out precision/coverage are modest and algorithm-dependent. HDH-only has no `L-S`. MC-only spans its stress levels and retains `L-S`; interpret stress as associated with the paired context, not as a randomized dose.
- **Common-stress combination views (25%, 50%, 75%):** compare the supported configurations at each shared stress setting with clients pooled. The 25% family contains seven combinations; 50% and 75% contain six and omit the triple-pattern combination. These are separate overlapping views. `L-S` held-out support rises across those selected slices (4, 7, 23 test targets), but box performance varies: precision/coverage are 0.33/0.75, 0.33/0.14, and 0.72/0.57 respectively.

Configuration allocation is established by each notebook’s Section 5 composite contingency outputs. Pattern counts or marginal ON/OFF summaries combine distinct configurations and contexts. The guide explicitly identifies shared/overlapping rows across campaign families; counts must not be summed as if the notebooks partition the data. All-environment means the sampled environment mix, not a controlled environment.

## RQ2 — Predictive joint conditions and guidance

The clearest recurring RQ2 signal is that `L-S` boxes often select low-delay observations; the 8- and 10-client all-configuration cases combine low delay with low HDH instances, while the 4-client case uses a different conjunction. Stress-only and profile slices surface stress, client-count, and task-context features, as expected from their scopes. Weighted importance is model-relative: ordering shifts by slice, while correlated proxies and fixed-scope exclusions make ranks non-transferable. Candidate predictors are not effects.

Held-out performance varies substantially. Baseline and MC-only are especially strong for the L-S rule in their own pooled profiles (perfect precision and coverage on 9/9 held-out targets in each), but these are narrow task-specific observations and MC-only’s condition is a stress envelope, not a causal compressor benefit. In the fixed client-count views, L-S precision is 1.0 in the reported PRIM rule, but coverage differs by client count. CS-only stress and combined-stress cases show lower precision or coverage; the absent HDH-only target makes evaluation unavailable. High precision with small retained support is limited evidence. Each case’s box report gives method-specific prevalence, retained count, precision, lift, and target coverage.

The policy-specific Section 11 matrices describe changes within each displayed policy group after filtering; their unrestricted comparison is not a universal baseline, and zero cells can be unsupported. They complement the held-out target-box summary rather than replacing it. The boxes are predictive scenario rules that can guide follow-up designs; they do not prove that imposing conditions will cause a target or reduce variance.

## What transfers and what does not

Within the text task, the outcome preference and common bin interpretation transfer across these slices. Counts, rules, and measured predictive performance do not transfer automatically across client counts, configuration profiles, stress-only views, or common-stress families. Cross-case agreement is strongest as a hypothesis that low delay is associated with the desired accuracy/time region; the policy contribution remains entangled with operating context and uneven sample composition.

**Practitioner implication:** use a case-specific box as a testable design/operating envelope only when it has held-out target support, adequate target capture, and useful precision; collect targeted repetitions under matched conditions before deployment decisions.

**Research implication:** evaluate candidate envelopes in a crossed or controlled follow-up with repeated grouped splits, explicit configuration-by-context support, and a new holdout. Add per-client/round diagnostics before transferring these run-level patterns to heterogeneous deployments.
