## RQ1 — Which tradeoffs occur, and how do design choices relate to them?

Across client-count cases and all focal-pattern views, observed support concentrates in L-S (high accuracy, short time) and L-M (high accuracy, medium time); M-S and M-M are sparse, L-L appears in the 4-client and HDH stress views, and the remaining grid cells are unobserved. Profile cases make the strongest stable within-slice contrast: the no-pattern and MC-only samples contain L-S/L-M, while CS-only adds sparse M-S and HDH-only is dominated by L-M with a small L-L tail. These are separate single-configuration profiles, not matched estimates of pattern effects. In all-configuration slices, complete policy allocation is more informative than ON/OFF marginals; client count is fixed within each c4/c8/c10 notebook. Pattern activation and derived pattern counts must not be conflated.

Stress leaves retain the named pattern ON, vary the other two decisions, and pool all three client counts. These are overlapping views, so their rows cannot be summed as unique campaign support. Each conditions on its own feature mix: CS stress suppresses `low_spec_percentage` as an exact alias, MC stress excludes `delay_percentage` as an alias, and HDH stress excludes `non_iid_percentage`; profile notebooks similarly omit duplicate pattern-stress aliases. The observed region allocations therefore mix the remaining configuration choices with sampled environment conditions. Across cases, STARR summaries show L-S and L-M are the most represented outcomes, while rare medium-accuracy and L-L regions have weaker membership stability. Empty cells are sample gaps, not feasibility boundaries.

## RQ2 — Which joint conditions identify targets, and what changes inside the box?

For L-S, low recorded delay is a recurring condition in the c4/c8 and profile cases, and MC-stress rules additionally select very low compressor stress; CS/HDH stress rules also include stress and context combinations. For L-M, high delay recurs across profiles and client-count leaves, often jointly with low or not-selected CS stress and run-level pattern-instance constraints. In joint-config slices, the rules also select client count, non-IID summaries, Dirichlet concentration, and pattern activation/context combinations. These are associations over the complete conjunction. A repeated threshold is a useful candidate for further evaluation, not an independently identified influence.

The better-supported L-S/L-M boxes generally retain pure target rows in held-out samples, but coverage differs substantially: client-count L-S boxes capture only about 19–65% of target runs; c4/c8 L-M rules capture about 57%, while c10 captures about 85%. Focal stress L-S boxes capture roughly 35–36% under PRIM, and stress L-M coverage is about 37–40%. Profile L-S/L-M rules often retain every held-out row in those two-class slices, so their enrichment over baseline is uninformative about selectivity: their exact delay partition mirrors the outcome split in the observed sample. Rare medium-accuracy and L-L boxes are fragile; several have no held-out target or capture only one to five target runs. Section 11 policy matrices refine membership by complete configuration and must be read with retained counts and coverage, not interpreted from zero cells alone.

## What transfers, and what does not

The robust cross-case observation is the dominance of high-accuracy outcomes, with the L-S/L-M split tracking observed delay ranges in simpler configuration profiles. Rules from all-configuration leaves do not transfer automatically across client counts; stress rules do not transfer to all-pattern settings because the other pattern choices and stress distributions vary. The profile leaves cannot compare patterns directly, and focal stress slices overlap. Held-out evaluation across only three seed groups per notebook is limited; compact client averages conceal individual-client and round-level variation.

- **Practitioners:** Use delay and joint policy/context rules to identify candidate operating envelopes for additional runs, with special focus on scarce medium-accuracy and long-time outcomes. Confirm precision, target coverage, and retained rows in the relevant policy group before using a rule for configuration selection.
- **Researchers:** Expand seed and client support for sparse regions, and design controlled or matched comparisons if estimating pattern contrasts. Preserve the distinction between complete configurations, marginal ON/OFF summaries, and derived instance counts; report alias exclusions and held-out support.
