# Image task synthesis — RQ1 and RQ2

Scope: CIFAR-10/CNN 16k, all sampled environments. The seven reports cover three all-configuration client-count slices (4, 8, 10) and four all-client-count configuration profiles (baseline and one pattern at a time). They are distinct analyses; single-pattern profiles are not direct substitutes for client-count-specific comparisons. The bin grid is shared campaign-wide: accuracy is maximized and average total round time minimized. See the [campaign README](../../README.md) and [refreshed evidence manifest](evidence-manifest.json).

## RQ1 — Which tradeoffs occur, and how do design choices relate to them?

### Recurring findings

Across cases, objective regions are categorized as accuracy-bin × round-time-bin, with nine possible cells. The partition counts in each case report show observed occupancy and zeros separately; empty cells denote missing sample support, not impossible outcomes. No configuration was deterministic in the notebook's contingency diagnostics. Results are associations across complete configurations and sampled environment mixes, not causal pattern effects. All-environment samples are not fully crossed, and the campaign README documents uneven environmental coverage.

Across all seven image slices, observed runs occupy only the S/M accuracy bins and S/M round-time bins: no run reaches an L accuracy bin or L round-time bin. M-M is the largest cell in every all-configuration client-count slice (c4: 187/273 train and 79/117 test; c8: 145/273 and 62/117; c10: 146/273 and 62/117). Its test share is therefore 67.5% at c4 versus 53.0% at c8 and c10. M-S is comparatively prominent at c4 (30/117 test) versus c8 (18/117) and c10 (16/117), while S-M is more prominent at c8/c10 (31 and 33 test runs) than c4 (4). These are descriptive contrasts between slices, not client-count effects. Across the all-client-count profiles, baseline has S-M/M-S/M-M; CS-only adds S-S; HDH-only has S-S/S-M/M-S/M-M in train but no held-out S-S/M-S; MC-only is dominated by M-M (111/147 train; 47/63 held out). Empty L bins mark no observed support in this campaign.

### Configuration/client-specific findings

The c4/c8/c10 cases are the appropriate slices for comparing complete configurations at a fixed client count. Their report tables provide all train/test region counts and each notebook's contingency, robustness and pattern-stress diagnostics. The baseline and CS-only, MC-only and HDH-only cases pool 4/8/10 clients; they describe configuration profiles and cannot be compared as if client count were fixed. `Pattern Types Active` is the number of ON decisions; `Pattern Instances` varies with participation and run context, so neither marginal stress grouping replaces a complete-configuration allocation. The image HDH caveat is material: the README reports HDH activity on only 48 of 1,371 participating non-IID clients in detailed image runs; configured HDH should not be described as consistently applied.

### Evidence gaps

The environment design is uneven and does not isolate policy from condition composition. Ten partition seeds give limited support for rare regions. Compact numeric client features are run-level aggregates and suppress client/round variation. Pattern instance estimates depend on assumptions; see [README stress caveats](../../README.md#5-pattern-stress).

## RQ2 — Which joint conditions identify a target tradeoff, and what changes inside the box?

### Recurring findings

Feature rankings differ by slice and are model-relative predictive credit. PRIM/CART conditions should be read jointly, keeping architectural decisions distinct from context and observed run descriptors. The seven box reports enumerate exact rules, discovery support, held-out prevalence, retained runs, precision, coverage and absolute lift; the notebooks' Section 11 matrices additionally distinguish configuration policy groups from the unrestricted test population. Box restriction filters observed test rows; it does not enact a configuration change. Missing target support makes evaluation unavailable, and a perfect precision on a small retained set is narrow evidence.

Alpha Dirichlet Mean and delay recur among the top-ranked features in c4/c8/c10 and baseline; the individual boxes also repeatedly use delay and alpha together. Low delay (about ≤12.5%) recurs in M-S boxes in baseline and the fixed-client slices, while M-M boxes often pair higher alpha with longer delay. This is a recurring joint association, not a direction-of-effect claim. Selector criteria, CPU and JSD enter some client-count boxes, showing that operating envelopes change by slice. Feature rank and box membership are separate signals: a high-ranked feature need not appear in every target rule.

### Evidence gaps

Some target regions have few or no held-out target runs; report-level metrics must be interpreted with the target denominator, retained support and coverage. A target absent from test is not evidence that the discovered condition is invalid or that the outcome is impossible. NaN/not-selected states matter where a box includes them; the per-case reports cite Section 12 diagnostics. Cross-case differences may reflect client-count slice, policy composition, environmental mix, or partition support. Additional seeds and controlled or balanced condition coverage are needed to test transfer and disentangle design from context.

## Conclusions and implications

For this image campaign, the strongest defensible conclusion is descriptive: the observed accuracy/time regions and joint operating conditions vary across configuration/client slices, while the present observational design does not identify causal pattern effects. Practitioners can use a case-specific box as a shortlist of conditions to validate, provided they preserve its scope and support. Researchers should prioritize additional grouped seeds, balanced environment coverage, and direct reporting of HDH activity and client-level/round-level measurements before claiming stable operating envelopes or transfer across configurations.
